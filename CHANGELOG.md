All notable changes to this project are documented here. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/) and the project uses [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/).

## [Unreleased]
### Added
- Chip in and Chat open as a sheet over the post (#302, owner's ask). They slide up over the post, and pulling one down, or tapping its ×, slides it back off to the post. The post still pulls down to the same feed ticket.
- The post sheet reads more like Bumble (#295, owner's ask). The cover photo fills the first screen edge to edge and fades into the field above the title. The facts are emoji pills: the category, 🙏 Need or 🎁 Want, 🎯 goal, and 📍 city for the ask, then ⭐ level, 🤝 people helped, 📅 joined, and ✅ follow-through in the poster card. Chip in is now a round gift button at the bottom right, like a dating app's like button, and it stays there instead of stepping aside. Chip in has its own page (`/posts/<id>/chip-in`) with the ask in brief at the top, so the post page no longer carries the form. Gift errors, "I sent it", and card cancels return there. The post shows ⏳ Open until as a pill. Next to the gift button, a chat button opens a mock chat (`/posts/<id>/chat`): a sample thread labeled as a preview, with a typing box that doesn't send. Real chat waits on #124 to #127. The poster gets neither page. On a wide screen the feed keeps its Chip in panel.
- A results update can carry one optional photo (#69, PB-20, as ADR 0008 planned). It goes through the same checks as post photos: up to 8 MB, JPG, PNG, or WebP, rebuilt as WebP with location data removed. The form says a receipt is never needed. It shows under the update. The poster and admins can remove it on its own, which leaves the post's photos in place. Deleting the post or account, removing the post, and bans delete it with the rest. Migration 0025 adds `posts.update_photo_key`. On the form, the photo is a single box like the new-post grid's. Under the update it fills the panel's width, so a large photo no longer makes phones scroll sideways.
- The README has the steps for serving photos from our own domain, `photos.yearnly.app` (#289, PB-48). No code change is needed: photo addresses and the CSP both follow `R2_PUBLIC_URL`.
- The post sheet shows the whole person, Bumble-style (#282, owner's picks). In order: the cover with the title and progress, the story, the category (with its emoji), Need or Want and the goal, photo 2, a poster card (name, city, joined date, a new short bio, follow-through, level, people helped, badges), photos 3 to 5, then the results update and Chip in. Visitors get a Chip in button that jumps to the panel. The bio is set in Settings: up to 300 characters, with no fundraising words, and deleting an account clears it. Migration 0024 adds `users.bio`. Look: the story and poster card are white bubbles, the facts are pills (the category in the ask's color), the extra photos are full width, and the floating Chip in sits bottom right and steps aside while the panel is on screen.
- The Post tab lists your posts, with **Create new post** at the top to open the questions (#278, owner's ask). At the 2-open-post limit it says why instead. The tab stays highlighted on the questions too. Create new post is a full-width green pill with the post icon on a phone, and an empty list no longer repeats the link.
- Up to five photos per post, the first one required (#279, ADR 0020, owner's ask). The new-post photo step has five boxes that preview what you pick. The first photo is the cover on the feed, and the post page shows the rest. Remove photos, deletes, admin removal, and bans take them all. Posts made before this are unchanged. Migration 0023 adds `posts.more_photo_keys`. The boxes are a photo grid like a dating app's: portrait tiles with a green + and a Cover tag on the first.
- Creating a post asks one question per screen, like Hinge or Bumble onboarding (#275, owner's pick): need or want, what it's for (category pills), title, story, goal, an optional photo, and the rules on a first post. A progress bar runs along the top, picking an option moves on by itself, and a server error opens on the question to fix. Without JS it's still one form. Validation and the edit page are unchanged. Each question is set big in League Gothic (owner's pick). Need or want are full-width rows with a box on the right that ticks, categories are emoji pills that fill green, and a round green → sits at the foot in thumb reach, with a round ← and Skip on the left. Questions slide in from the side you're heading to, and on a phone they fill the screen between the bars.
- No platform fee, and an optional tip (#265, ADR 0019, amending ADR 0016). `PLATFORM_FEE_RATE` now defaults to 0, so the only cut of a card gift is Stripe's card fee. On the website, card checkout offers "Tip to support Yearnly" (none by default, $1, $2, or up to $50) as a separate line. Yearnly absorbs Stripe's fee on it, so the recipient gets the same, and the tip never counts toward a post or rewards. The iOS shell adds `YearnlyApp` to its user agent, and there the tip is left out and refused on the server. The Terms are updated.
- Settings won't remove your last way to get paid while you have an open post, or one waiting for review (#21, owner's pick): "Keep one way to get paid while you have an open post, or close the post first." Swapping handles and card payouts (Stripe) still count.
- `flask --app app auth prune` deletes login and email throttle rows older than a week, which is far past every limit's window. It runs in the daily job (#15). Email was already sent off the request path (#105).
- Monitoring (#259, PB-15, owner's picks). With `SENTRY_DSN` set, unhandled errors go to Sentry, scrubbed of request bodies, cookies, headers, query strings, users, and link tokens. Database errors hide row values. The README covers the UptimeRobot check on `/health` and Neon's point-in-time restore. New dependency: `sentry-sdk[flask]`.
- Failed photo deletes are retried (#149, PB-51, owner's pick: a to-delete list). If storage fails when a photo is removed, its key waits in `photo_deletes`, and the daily job's `flask --app app posts retry-photos` tries again until it's gone. It needs the R2 secrets and otherwise skips. Migration 0022 adds the table.
- A public Support page at `/support` (#264), linked from the footer, for the App Store listing. It covers how to email support@yearnly.app, report or block someone, where payment problems go, password reset, account deletion, and links to Rules, Terms, and Privacy.
- The reminder emails run every day on their own (#258, PB-35). A GitHub Actions workflow runs `gifts remind` and `posts remind` at 15:00 UTC against Neon and Brevo, using the `DATABASE_URL` and `BREVO_API_KEY` repository secrets, and skips with a warning until they're set.
- Block another user (#257, App Store Guideline 1.2). Block from someone's profile or post, and unblock from Settings → Blocked. A block works both ways: neither of you sees the other's posts in the feed or can open, chip in on, or react to them. It's silent, so they're never told. Deleting an account drops its blocks, and the Privacy page lists them. Migration 0021 adds `blocks`.

### Security
- When a password reset confirms an email for the first time, the account's payment handles and city are cleared and the owner is told, so a handle saved by whoever signed up with that email first can't collect the owner's gifts (#133).
- Resetting a password now logs out every session on that account, so someone who signed up with another person's email and stayed logged in loses access once the real owner claims it through a reset link. Logging in or signing up also starts a fresh session (#130, migration 0016). Everyone gets logged out once when this deploys.
- Password reset links no longer carry the account's password hash. Signed links can be read by anyone who sees them, so a forwarded or logged reset email could have been cracked offline. Links now hold a keyed digest instead, and still work only once (#134). Reset links sent before this deploys stop working; ask for a new one.
- Sign-up is limited to 10 new accounts an hour from one internet address, so nobody can make a farm of accounts or use up the daily email quota with verification emails to strangers. A household or campus network sharing an address still has room (#137).
- A ban now hides the user everywhere: all their posts (closed and funded ones too) and photos are removed, so gifts to them stop counting; they drop off every leaderboard and stop counting toward who gets the top of the feed; their profile is 404 to everyone but admins; and they can no longer be reported (#147).
- When an admin removes a post, or bans a user and so removes their open posts, the photos are deleted too. Before, the post was gone but its photo link still worked (#144).
- The admin queue flags possible fake helping: a donor where 3 or more of the people they helped got nothing from anyone outside that group (banned accounts don't count as outside) and joined within a day of each other. Each flag shows what the donor gave each account, and admins can ban the donor alone or the donor and the flagged accounts together. Flags never change the leaderboard, and gifts of any size still count (#140).
- The session cookie is now Secure on HTTPS (decided from `APP_URL`) and SameSite=Lax everywhere. Every page sends a strict Content Security Policy (scripts, styles, and fonts from the site only, photos from the site or R2, forms only to the site or Stripe), refuses to be framed, and sends nosniff and a same-origin referrer policy. HTTPS deployments also send HSTS for a year (#142).
- Links in emails and the return links sent to Stripe are now built from `APP_URL`, not the address the request came in on, so a forged Host header can't send someone a reset or verify link pointing at another site (#150).
- The app refuses to start unless `SECRET_KEY` is a random value of at least 32 characters and `APP_URL` is https. Only clear development (`FLASK_ENV=development`, debug mode, or tests) can skip this, so a deploy that forgets a variable fails loudly instead of running insecurely. Before, a deploy missing the key would have made sessions and email links forgeable, and one missing `APP_URL` would have sent http://localhost links with an insecure cookie (#152).
- One slow request no longer stalls the whole site: staging runs gunicorn with 4 threads (one process, to fit the free plan's memory) and a 60-second timeout. Photos decode one at a time, and JPEGs decode at reduced size. Request bodies are capped at 1 MB except on the new-post form, which keeps 9 MB for a photo (#155).
- The fundraising word check now sees through disguises: look-alike letters from other alphabets, accents, hidden characters, digits for letters ("bus1ness"), and spaced-out letters ("b.u.s.i.n.e.s.s"). Posts are also checked for company, funding, capital, profit, return on, angel, shop, and "my brand" (not "my brand new"), with Los Angeles, Capital One, "stock up", and non-profit left alone; a match still only sends the post to review. Results updates, which are refused rather than reviewed, keep the original shorter list, and the message now names the word (#158).
- A verification link now confirms an email only in a browser logged in to that account. - Deleting an account now also clears its password-reset and verification-email throttle rows, so a deleted user's email address doesn't stay in the database (#169).
- Logging out now clears the whole session, so nothing from the visit (a saved way back, the form token) carries over to whoever uses the browser next (#171).
- Admins can be removed with `flask --app app admin revoke <email>`. Every admin action (approve, remove a post or photo, ban, dismiss, grant, revoke) is recorded with who did it and when, and /admin lists the 25 most recent. Admin pages return 404 for ids too large for the database instead of an error (#173, migration 0017).
- Each account can file 10 reports a day, and admins get at most one report email an hour, timed from the last email sent. Later reports wait in the queue, so one account can't flood the queue or the admins' inboxes (#175).
- Deploys install exact, hash-checked dependency versions from a new `requirements.lock`, the same ones the tests run against, instead of whatever is newest. CI audits that lock, scans the whole git history for secrets with a checksum-verified gitleaks, pins its Actions to commit hashes, and runs with a read-only token (#177).
- Failed logins typed as an account's internal throttle name no longer count against that account's three verification emails an hour, so a stranger can't hold back someone's verification email (#179).
